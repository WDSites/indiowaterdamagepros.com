Flood damage restoration is the work of getting storm runoff, groundwater or sewage-tainted water out of a building and returning it to a dry, sanitary condition. It differs from a plumbing leak because flood water carries silt, bacteria and debris, so it is usually treated as Category 2 or Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

A same-day crew visit is possible when someone is free, including after hours. Tell the dispatcher where the water entered and how deep it is, and they will explain what to do next.

When floodwater is an emergency

Flood losses are urgent for three reasons. The water itself is contaminated, it spreads quickly into porous materials, and a house that stays wet is a house that starts to grow mold. Treat it as an emergency when water has entered living areas, when it has risen near outlets, or when it carries mud, sewage or yard runoff.

After-hours calls get the same attention as daytime calls. Crews can be dispatched at night and on weekends, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Safety first: electricity and contaminated water

Do not wade through water that touches outlets, extension cords or appliances. Shut off power at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it from dry ground, and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line.

Floodwater and sewage should not touch bare skin. Keep children and pets out of the area until a crew has removed the water and sanitized the surfaces.

How flood damage differs from a pipe leak

A burst supply line sends clean water into one part of a house. Flood water comes from outside or from a drain, travels across yards and driveways and picks up soil, bacteria and chemicals on the way. The S500 standard classifies it as Category 2 or 3, which changes the plan.

Porous materials that soaked up Category 3 water are usually removed and discarded rather than dried in place. That means carpet and pad, insulation and the lower section of drywall, often cut away above the visible waterline. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, and the structure is dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until moisture meters confirm it is dry.

Flood history in and around Indio

Tropical Storm Hilary struck Southern California from August 19 to 21, 2023. A FEMA public notice posted in November 2024 says floodwaters eroded the Coachella Valley Stormwater Channel and damaged the City of Indio's Westward Ho water main, in an area mapped as a Special Flood Hazard Area. Riverside County proclaimed a local emergency and reported flooded neighborhoods across the region.

More recently, local TV reported flash flooding in the Indio Hills area on September 28, 2026, with Dillon Road closed for several hours and a flash flood warning that also covered Indio. That event was north of the city, so it was not a flood inside Indio itself, but it shows how quickly desert washes can fill.

The practical takeaway is to look up your address at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, stack sandbags at doors and low points before a storm, and keep a record of your home's low spots. The City of Indio storm page recommends these steps.

Basement flooding cleanup

If your home has a basement, crawl space, garage low spot or sunken room, that area collects water first and holds it longest. Standing water there is pumped out with submersible pumps, then the space is cleaned and the structure is dried.

Crews remove wet insulation, carpet and the lower part of any finished walls, then run air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture readings fall into the dry range. Pumping too fast from a space with saturated soil outside can stress the walls, so a crew lowers the water in stages.

Restoration is not waterproofing. A restoration crew can dry the space and rebuild it, but keeping water out needs drainage work, grading or a sump system from a different trade. The crew can tell you what they saw while they were on site.

Sewage coming up a drain or toilet?

Gurgling drains, sewage smell, or dirty water rising in a floor drain, tub or toilet usually points to a blockage or a problem in the sewer line. The trade name for the cleanup is sewage backup cleanup, and it begins with keeping everyone away from the water.

Sewage is Category 3 water. Crews wear protective gear, extract the water, remove porous materials that touched it, then clean and disinfect hard surfaces. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the structure, and HEPA air scrubbers can help with airborne particles in some jobs.

In Indio, sewer service is run by the Valley Sanitary District, so report the backup to them while you call for cleanup. If the blockage is in your own lateral line, a plumber has to clear it before the cleanup can be finished.

Flood insurance and your claim

Flood is the one kind of water damage that homeowners policies usually leave out. The California Department of Insurance says flood insurance is sold as a separate policy, and policies generally take effect 30 days after purchase. Sudden pipe failures and other non-flood water damage may fall under your homeowners policy, so the cause of the water matters.

Ask your insurer how your loss is classified before you assume anything, and keep a log of calls and paperwork. The crew can document moisture readings and photograph damaged materials as they are removed, which helps with whichever policy applies.

What happens after extraction
