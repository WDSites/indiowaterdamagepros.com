Water Damage Restoration in Trilogy at The Polo Club, Indio, CA

Guidance for owners in Trilogy at The Polo Club, a newer Shea Homes community in Indio, including what changes when your home shares a wall with a neighbor.

☎ Call (760) 769-1159

Trilogy at The Polo Club is a newer community in Indio, about 1.3 miles from the Empire Polo Club according to a trade press article about the development. Newer construction brings a different set of questions than an older neighborhood, and some of them depend on whether your home is detached or attached.

If water is spreading, make the area safe and call the number on this page. Tell the person who answers whether you own a detached home, a semi-detached home or a condominium, because that changes who else may be affected.

What sources say about the community

Shea Homes is the developer, and a regional trade publication describes the community as having an all ages section and a 55 and older section, with floor plans from about 1,294 square feet to more than 2,300 square feet. A real estate guide says construction began around 2013 and includes single-family homes, semi-detached homes and resort collection condominiums.

Sales figures differ by source, and we have not verified roof, piping or slab details for individual homes. Treat the numbers here as general descriptions, not official counts.

When your home shares a wall

Water does not respect a property line. A leak in one unit can travel through a shared wall, a ceiling or a floor and reach the neighbor, who may not be home. If you find a stain on a shared wall, tell the neighbor and the association early.

Attached homes also raise a question of who is responsible for what. The association documents and each owner's policy decide that, so keep copies handy and photograph both sides of the wall when you can.

Newer homes still leak

Newer plumbing and appliances reduce some risks, but not all. Supply lines, ice maker connections, dishwasher hoses and water heater fittings can fail at any age. Installation errors can show up in the first years, which is worth mentioning to the builder or association if your home is still under a builder warranty.

Ask what warranty covers before a repair, because moving or removing a damaged part can complicate a later claim. Photograph first.

Safety steps first

Keep away from standing water around outlets and appliances, and do not stand in water to reach the breaker panel. If it is safe, shut off water at the fixture or main valve. Call 911 for fire, gas or any danger to life.

Keep children and pets out of the affected area. Treat water from a drain, toilet or outdoors as contaminated and leave it for trained crews.

Questions to answer when you call

Having these ready shortens the first conversation.

  • Is your home detached, semi-detached or a condominium?
  • Which rooms and floors are wet?
  • Could the water have reached a neighbor?
  • Has the water stopped, and where did it start?
  • Is there a gate or guest access step?

How drying is done

Crews extract standing water, remove materials that cannot recover and set air movers and dehumidifiers. Readings are taken each visit and drying ends when the structure reads dry. Hard floors over a slab, tile and drywall each respond differently.

Read about structural drying and water damage cleanup for details.

Insurance and association paperwork

Keep your photos, a timeline, repair receipts and any message from the association together. For an attached home, ask which policy responds to the shared elements and which responds to your contents. Coverage depends on the policy and the cause, and no outcome is promised here.
