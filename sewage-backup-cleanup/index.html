If sewage is coming up a drain, tub or toilet, stop using every fixture, keep people and pets out and call the 24/7 line. Do not wade in, and do not try to flush or run water to clear it. The water is contaminated and the exposure risk is real.

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of contaminated water and the materials it soaked, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and drying. It differs from a clean supply leak because the water is Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, so porous materials are generally removed. For stormwater and groundwater, see flood damage restoration.

How sewage backs up in a home

A backup happens when wastewater cannot flow away and the lowest opening in the house takes the overflow. That is usually a floor drain, a shower or a tub, sometimes a toilet. A blockage in the house line, a root intrusion, a collapsed pipe or a problem in the main line can all do it.

The key question is which line is blocked. If only your fixtures back up, the blockage is probably in your own line and a plumber can clear it. If several neighbors have trouble or fixtures in more than one area back up at once, the main may be involved. The Valley Sanitary District serves Indio and nearby areas for sewer, and its website has a Report a Problem page.

Cleanup is the same either way, but the repair call is different. Plumbers handle the house line. The district handles the main. Tell the dispatcher what you know, and report the problem to the district while the crew handles the contaminated area.

Sewage safety rules for the first hour

Sewage can carry bacteria and other harmful agents. Take these steps and no more.

  1. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and washers. Every drop you add goes to the same line.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with health concerns away from the affected area.
  3. Do not touch the water or the soaked items with bare hands. Wear rubber gloves and boots if you must move.
  4. Leave electrical devices alone. Turn power off at the panel only from dry ground.
  5. Open windows near the area if you can do so without walking through the water.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and report the problem to the district.

How crews remove the sewage and make the space safe again

Crews wear protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators. They extract the water with pumps and extraction units, then remove porous materials that held it, such as carpet, pad, insulation and the lower part of drywall. These items are bagged and removed, not cleaned.

Cleaning and sanitizing

Hard surfaces that remain are cleaned first, because sanitizer cannot work through soil. Then an antimicrobial product is applied according to its label. HEPA air scrubbers filter particles during demolition, and odor control follows once the source materials are gone.

Drying and records

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map what is left wet. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged each visit. When walls and floors are rebuilt, water damage cleanup and water damage repair explain the next steps.

What changes the cost of sewage backup cleanup

The amount of contaminated material and the number of rooms are the biggest factors. A backup in a bathroom is a smaller scope than one that reaches a hallway and bedrooms. The floor type matters, because carpet and pad come out and tile can be cleaned in place.

How long the water sat adds steps. So does the need to cut drywall and remove insulation. Protective gear and disposal add to the work because contaminated material is handled more carefully than ordinary debris. Clearing the line is plumbing and is a separate cost.

Insurance and sewage backups

Coverage for sewage backup varies. Some policies treat sewer or drain backup separately from other water damage, so read your policy language and ask your insurer. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage, which is why the source of the water matters to the claim.

The department also advises making temporary repairs to prevent further damage and keeping a log of calls and paperwork. Photos before removal, a list of discarded items and the written scope help your adjuster.

Mistakes to avoid and how to keep it from returning

Do not try to clean sewage with a household vacuum or a mop and bucket. Do not run a fan across the area, which can spread contamination. Do not keep carpet or pad that touched sewage, even if it looks clean once dry.

Prevention depends on the line. Do not pour grease down drains and do not flush wipes. Have the house line scoped if backups repeat, and ask a plumber about a backwater valve. Keep floor drains clear and the trap primed.

California requires a state contractor license for plumbing work above a set dollar value, according to the Contractors State License Board.
