Water damage restoration in Indian Wells means stopping the water, removing what is standing, drying the structure and rebuilding what cannot be saved. It is the same sequence for a house near Fred Waring Drive and for a casita inside a golf community.

Desert seasons and where Indian Wells water problems start

The low desert has two water seasons. Summer is the long one, when air conditioners run for months and every condensate line, drain pan and supply hose works harder than it does anywhere else in the year. A clogged condensate line can drip into a ceiling for weeks before a stain shows.

Storms are the short season. Thunderstorms and the occasional tropical remnant can drop a lot of rain in a few hours on ground that is dry and hard, so water runs off instead of soaking in. Tropical Storm Hilary in August 2023 is the recent example in this valley, and a FEMA notice documents damage to a water main in Indio from that storm.

The practical tie-back is timing. In summer, check the air handler closet and the ceiling below it before you leave for a long trip. After a storm, check the garage, the lowest door threshold and any wall that faces the slope of the lot.

When water is spreading in Indian Wells

Water that is still moving gets worse by the minute. Drywall wicks upward, baseboards swell and carpet pad holds water like a sponge. The first hour matters more than anything a crew does later.

Find the source and stop it. For a supply line or water heater, that is the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main valve at the house. If the source is a roof leak or storm water, move belongings off the floor and call the 24/7 line so a crew can start emergency water removal as soon as one is available.

Answer the dispatcher's questions about what is wet, how long it has been wet and whether the water is clean, gray or from a sewer line. That tells the crew which equipment to bring. We offer same-day response when available, and the dispatcher will tell you honestly what to expect.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading in Indian Wells

These steps are safe for most homeowners and buy time. Skip any step that puts you near standing water and electricity.

Stop the source

Close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve. A condensate overflow or irrigation break can be stopped at the unit or the controller.

Power off wet areas only if safe

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor. If you cannot, stay out and call the utility or 911 for any electrical risk.

Lift what you can

Raise furniture legs onto blocks, pull rugs and lift valuables, books and electronics to a dry surface.

Keep people and pets out

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a sewer line, a toilet or the outdoors.

Photograph before you move things

Take wide and close photos of the damage and the source. Your insurer will ask for them.

Call the 24/7 line

Dispatch is answered around the clock. The sooner drying equipment is running, the less material has to be thrown away.

Golf community homes, resort casitas and homes that sit empty

That mix matters for water damage in two ways. Many homes in resort and club communities sit unoccupied during the hottest months, so a failed supply line or water heater can run for days before anyone notices. And a leak in a multi-unit building can reach the unit below, which raises the question of who is responsible for the repair.

If you manage a second home here, ask a neighbor or property manager to walk through weekly in summer. If water reaches a shared wall or ceiling, call us and your association together, because the cause often sits in a shared system. Our water mitigation service covers the drying and moisture mapping that both parties' insurers will want documented.

A restoration crew handles water inside the property. A main break in the street or a sewer problem in the lateral outside your house usually belongs to the utility, so call them as well.

Permit rules differ by job. The City of Indian Wells takes building permit applications online through its Permit Hub. Ask the city whether your repair needs one, and ask your contractor to confirm that the work is licensed. Any job of $1,000 or more in labor and materials needs a California contractor license.

Insurance and documentation after a loss

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer, because wording differs.

Whatever the cause, the crew's moisture readings, photos and scope of work help your claim. In a homeowners association community, also ask the association what its master policy covers.
